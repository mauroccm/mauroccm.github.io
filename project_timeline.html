Project Timeline

A chronological account of milestones, discoveries, and future directions in the study of climate-sensitive vector-borne diseases.

Milestones

Jan 2025

Start of the project on climate-sensitive vector-borne diseases. Yellow fever outbreak in the State of São Paulo. This proposal aligns with the IPSP strategy.

Apr 2025

Shift to studying West Nile Virus (WNV) following the Climate and Health conveening in Hong Kong. Preparation of a project aimed at studying how climatic factors affect the distribution of arboviruses using machine learning.

May 2025

Vaccine refusal was the primary trigger for yellow fever cases, according to a report by the São Paulo State Health Secretariat.

Oct 2025

Article by Prof. William Souza's group (Lima et al., The Lancet Regional Health – Americas, 2025) on WNV circulation in Brazil and its relationship with the climate in Ceará (CE).

Nov 2025

How should Brazil address vaccine refusal? The question arose following a discussion with Juan Carlo Ocampo during the Global Health in the Amazon course — disbelief in climate change is a phenomenon psychologically similar to vaccine hesitancy.

Dec 2025

ANVISA approves the Butantan-DV vaccine for use in Brazil — a single-dose, live-attenuated preventive vaccine for individuals aged 12 to 59.

Jan – Feb 2026

Testing different modeling approaches for yellow fever risk and its association with temperature. The occurrence of 7 or more consecutive days with a minimum temperature above 17 °C between April and September showed the strongest association.

Mar 2026

While searching for studies on "climate hesitancy," found the work of Fujita et al. (2024), which identifies climate change as a factor responsible for shifts in the dengue serotype profile in Brazil.

Mar 2026

Following a meeting with COVISA (Eduardo Masi, arbovirus surveillance in the municipality of São Paulo), identified the need for a dynamic, high-resolution (meter-scale) mapping and alert system for dengue monitoring — distinct from InfoDengue which operates at the municipal level.

Apr 2026

Attended the One Health Summit in Lyon, France, and partnership with Leonard Heyerdahl (IP Paris).

May 2026

Submission of an opinion piece regarding a "flaw" in the Phase 3 clinical trial of the Butantan-DV vaccine. Director of Immunization program at PAHO has expressed interest in methods for "social listening" of vaccine hesitancy in Brazil.

Jun 2026

The Brazilian Ministry of Health temporarily suspends the dengue vaccination strategy using Butantan-DV as a preventive measure following the observation of a few rare, unexpected events.

Jul 2026

New yellow fever modeling using the INLA model; manuscript preparation for publication and abstract for PNAM. Butantan Foundation is interested in C&H project for dengue in IPSP.

Future Perspectives
Sep 2026

Scientific meeting to establish new partnerships between IPSP and IP Montevideo (approved by FAPESP).

Nov 2026

Participation in the PNAM (Pasteur Network Anual Meeting) event.

Open question

How can social listening be linked to high-resolution dengue modeling? These are scientifically distinct approaches, yet they share common themes such as machine learning, dengue, and prevention. Social listening on digital networks may not be effective this year, however, as federal elections are taking place in Brazil (president, governors, state and federal deputies, and senators), and state-owned entities such as the Butantan Institute will not post on social media until the official campaign period concludes.
